## How my posts are built

### The hook (line 1, the "title")

It has to stop the scroll **and** be literally true. The claim in line 1 must
be fully supported by the source cited for it, with no stretch in population,
size or certainty. If the honest version of the hook is weaker, use the honest
version.

Formulas from `hooks.json` that fit evidence-based posts:

- **first choice:** #11 Myth Bust, #2 Number Reveal (published figures only),
  #1 Contrarian Take (the contrarian part must be what the evidence says),
  #19 Curiosity Gap (closed by line 3 with the finding), #8 Question Trap (a
  realistic clinical scenario, never a real identifiable patient), #10 The
  Receipt, #15 The Warning (always with the fix), #16 Good vs Great (on study
  design or clinical practice), #18 The Unpopular Rule (a method rule I keep),
  #6 Insider Secret (what protocol work teaches that papers leave out);
- **with care:** #5 List Promise (7 items at most), #9 Story Cold Open (only a
  real, non-identifying line, with consent where needed), #12 Comparison (only
  where a head-to-head comparison exists), #21 Direct Value (generic tools,
  never company documents), #3 Mistake Confession (real methodological
  mistakes);
- **never:** #7 The Callout (no contempt for colleagues), #13 Permission Slip,
  #20 The Walk-Away, #17 Time Anchor (unless a published study measured the
  time), #14 Pattern Interrupt (once a month at most).

### The body

```
Line 1   hook: a verified fact or a real tension in the evidence
Line 2   the finding in one sentence
Body     what was looked at and in whom, in plain words
         what changed, and how much, without overstating it
         what we do not know yet (one line, when it changes the reading)
Turn     what it means for practice or for research
Close    one specific question for clinicians or researchers
```

Engaging comes from concrete scenes (the procedure, the ward, the moment of
the needle), real numbers and short sentences, never from adjectives or
promises.

### Evidence rules (non-negotiable, but invisible in the post)

The post does not need to cite its sources: no author names, journals or
links in the text unless Matteo asks. The evidence works behind the scenes.

1. Every factual claim comes from a peer-reviewed source, checked at the
   source on PubMed or Europe PMC **before** drafting: first author, journal,
   year, DOI or PMID. These go in the receipt under the draft, for Matteo,
   not in the post. A number with no source is cut, or comes back as
   `{{fonte da verificare}}`.
2. Never take figures or citations from the Lemons website, decks or
   marketing material without checking the primary paper: wrong PMIDs and
   misattributed figures have been found there.
3. Hierarchy: systematic reviews and meta-analyses, then randomised trials,
   then the rest. Pilot and observational studies are named as such.
4. Choose topics where the evidence supports an assertive statement, and
   state it as the authors did, in the population that was studied. A result
   in children's venipuncture is not evidence for adult chemotherapy.
5. Statistical significance is not clinical relevance: never let a
   significant but small effect read as a large one.
6. Results of our own studies only once published or publicly presented.
7. A first comment with the references is optional: suggest it in the
   receipt when a post makes a specific figure-based claim that could be
   challenged.
8. Posts are Lemons company communication (Matteo, 14 Oct 2026): write in
   assertive form and build each post on the evidence that supports it.
   Unfavourable or uncertain findings (e.g. adults, chemotherapy, quality
   of evidence) are left out rather than discussed. Limits that never move:
   every assertion matches what the source concluded, in the population it
   studied (children stay children); no efficacy claim about LEMO beyond its
   CE intended purpose (MDR art. 7); the register stays scientific
   information, not formal advertising (D.Lgs. 137/2022 art. 26; Codice
   deontologico art. 57).
9. When a post is about virtual reality, it makes clear that I work for
   Lemons in the Room (a closing line, or naturally in the text).

## My positions

Proposals drawn from Matteo's own documents. `{{da confermare o riscrivere}}`

1. Virtual reality in hospital is an intervention and must be measured like
   one: controlled designs, validated instruments, pre-specified endpoints,
   limits stated.
2. In children, VR during procedures rests on a solid body of studies: the
   next step is building the same evidence, setting by setting, with partner
   hospitals.
3. A small pilot run properly, 10 to 15 patients per centre, is worth more
   than a large one run carelessly.
4. Economic outcomes belong in the protocol from day one: a hospital adopts
   what it can evaluate.
5. Non-pharmacological care at the bedside deserves the same rigour as a drug.

## Off limits

- **Topics I do not post about:** patients or anything that could identify
  them; images of minors; internal company matters (team, prices, contracts,
  quality and incident registers); competitors.
- **Clients or numbers I cannot name publicly:** results of studies not yet
  published, including the Careggi gynaecological oncology study until the
  paper is out; enrolment figures; names of investigators, hospitals or ethics
  committees not yet confirmed or approved; prices.
- **Claims I am not allowed to make:**
  - Codice di Deontologia Medica (FNOMCeO 2014): art. 55, information must be
    rigorous and prudent and must not raise unfounded expectations; art. 56,
    no promotion of innovations not yet validated; art. 57, a physician does
    not lend his patronage to promotional advertising of health products.
    It applies to me as a registered physician whoever employs me: I can
    present LEMO as the company's Head of Clinical Research, but not as "the
    doctor who recommends it".
  - D.Lgs. 137/2022, art. 26: advertising a medical device to the public
    needs authorisation from the Ministry of Health; information for
    healthcare professionals does not, but online it belongs on channels
    reserved to them. LinkedIn is public. Information (news, studies, how
    LEMO is used, milestones) is fine; a post with a promotional aim (selling
    benefits, calls to buy, prices) is public advertising.
    `{{da verificare: Lemons ha un'autorizzazione ministeriale alla
    pubblicità di LEMO?}}`
  - Law 145/2018, para. 525, as amended by DL 69/2023: no attractive or
    suggestive elements in health communication.
  - MDR (EU 2017/745), art. 7: nothing about LEMO beyond its CE intended
    purpose.
  - FNOMCeO recommendations on social media, email and messaging (2023): not
    yet part of the Code, but the most concrete reference for a physician's
    social presence.
  - Therefore never: "scientificamente validata", "clinically proven",
    "reduces anxiety by X%" without the study it comes from, "replaces
    sedation or drugs", "drug-free", "side-effect-free", LEMO presented as a
    treatment, patient testimonials.
